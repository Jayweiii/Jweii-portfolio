As a Workshop Wizard and the 3D Printer Tool Lead at the California NanoSystems Institute, I am delighted to submit my application for the R\&D Intern position at Johnson \& Johnson. With my extensive experience in 3D printing, CAD, CNC machining, and high voltage systems design, I am confident in my ability to support Johnson \& Johnson's mission of overcoming healthcare challenges.

I have thorough machine shop and fabrication experience due to my role as a Workshop Wizard and 3D Printer Tool Lead at the California NanoSystems Institute (CNSI). I have managed, repaired, and improved processes for our Haas Super Mini Mill and all types of our 3D printers, including FDM, SLA, DLP, and PolyJet. I spend about 10 hours per week in the shop designing and manufacturing researchers' experiment setups. As a result, I have developed a thorough understanding of the different design and material considerations of additive manufacturing, subtractive manufacturing (CNC \& manual machining), chemical post-processes (such as acetone vapor smoothing), and even combining these different manufacturing methods in creating a single part.

At Gaucho Racing, I lead the design and manufacturing of our safe, modular, and repairable high voltage battery pack, drivetrain, and mounting solutions. Those criteria have been critical as it makes it easy to iterate quickly to address issues, such as strength or space constraints, that arise during testing. To tackle our design criterias most cost and time effectively, I have been able to utilize various manufacturing methods including CNC, welding, 3D printing, laser cutting, soldering, and resin bonding. Specifically, I am most proud of our carbon fiber filled 3D printed battery mounts, topology optimized steel differential manifold, and fiberglass wet layup composite. My current ambitions are to combine 3D printed geometries, such as gyroids, with phase-change thermofluidics, to increase the efficiency of heat exchangers. This will allow for the improved performance and increased operational temperature range for a variety of power electronics.

In conclusion, I am excited to bring the skills that I have accrued through hands-on engineering experiences to Johnson \& Johnson. Thank you for considering my application, and I look forward to discussing this internship opportunity and my qualifications in further detail.
